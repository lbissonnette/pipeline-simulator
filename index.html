    How the model works
    • Arrivals per tick are Poisson with the mean you set (optionally modulated by a slow sine wave). An upstream delay holds arrivals in a buffer and releases them all at once when it ends.
    • Bundling cuts a bundle whenever the intake buffer holds a full bundle's worth; a partial bundle is flushed after the configured wait so nothing sits forever at low traffic.
    • Routing sends each bundle to one worker. Least loaded picks the online worker with the fewest queued conversions (a health-aware balancer). Round robin and random keep sending to offline workers, like a static partition assignment would.
    • Processing: each tick every online worker draws a rate from the distribution, multiplies it by its permanent speed factor, and completes that fraction of a bundle's worth of conversions from the front of its queue. Spare capacity spills into the next bundle. Rates are clamped to 0–100% per tick.
    • Capacity is workers × mean rate × bundle size. When the load ratio ρ (arrivals ÷ capacity) is above 1 the backlog grows without bound; below 1 it stays bounded, but latency climbs steeply as ρ approaches 1.
    • Latency is measured per bundle from the tick its oldest conversion arrived to the tick the bundle finished.
