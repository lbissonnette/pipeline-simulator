    How the model works
    • Arrivals per tick are Poisson with the mean you set (optionally modulated by a slow sine wave). An upstream delay holds arrivals in a buffer and releases them all at once when it ends.
    • Bundling cuts a bundle whenever the intake buffer holds a full bundle's worth and the dispatcher has room; a partial bundle is flushed after the configured wait. When the dispatcher is full the bundler stops, and conversions accumulate in its buffer.
    • Dispatcher: a bounded FIFO of bundles. Each tick it hands bundles to idle workers. Any idle worker and round robin only use online workers; sticky partition assigns each bundle a worker up front and waits for that worker, even if it is busy or offline, like a static partition assignment.
    • Processing: each worker holds one bundle. Every tick it draws a rate from the distribution, multiplies by its permanent speed factor and any slowdown, clamps to 0–100%, and completes that fraction of a bundle. When the bundle is done the worker idles for the rest of the tick and takes the next bundle on the following tick.
    • Capacity = workers × bundle size ÷ expected ticks per bundle. The expected ticks are estimated by simulation, so they include the leftover capacity wasted at the end of each bundle. When the load ratio ρ (arrivals ÷ capacity) is above 1 the dispatcher pins at its capacity and the backlog grows without bound at the bundler.
    • Latency is measured per bundle from the tick its oldest conversion arrived to the tick the bundle finished, so it includes time spent waiting at the bundler and in the dispatcher.
